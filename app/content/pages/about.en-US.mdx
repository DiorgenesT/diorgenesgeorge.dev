## What I build

My full name is Diorgenes George Tavares Silva. I work at Fundação Beta, the public research and technology foundation of the city government of Betim, Brazil, as a senior analyst and software developer.

What comes out of there serves three very different audiences: the executive office, which needs trustworthy indicators to decide; the public employee, who needs to get things done without walking to a department; and the citizen, who needs information that used to exist only inside a government building.

In practice that means interfaces in React, Next.js and TypeScript, APIs in Python with FastAPI and SQLAlchemy, data processing with Pandas, and the whole infrastructure on Cloudflare — Workers, Pages, D1, KV and R2 — with PostgreSQL and Supabase as the data layer.

## Why public software is different

Government systems carry constraints commercial products do not. They answer to legislation on data protection, freedom of information and disability rights, so accessibility is a legal obligation rather than a finishing touch. They handle data that must not leak and must not be wrong: a wrong number on an executive dashboard is worse than no dashboard at all, because the decision becomes fiction wearing the appearance of data.

They also have to live alongside the process that already exists. The correct technical decision is often the worse one on paper. I have chosen to export documents in an editable format instead of PDF, because the real approval path runs through a signature on paper and an immutable format would stall the entire process. That kind of choice only shows up for people who have shipped software inside an organization, not for people who have only published an app.

## How I got here

I spent four years managing a customer support team before writing software professionally, tracking queue metrics and solving the problem of whoever was on the other end of the line. I finished my computer science degree in 2024 and joined Fundação Beta in November 2025.

I do not treat those four years as a detour. Much of what I build today is support work wearing different clothes: an emergency room queue a citizen can check before leaving home, an HR form that no longer requires a trip to the office. People who have worked the front line write software differently.

## How I work

I prefer an explained decision to an elegant one. Every system I ship carries the origin of the data it displays, because when a number is challenged you need to be able to walk back to the spreadsheet that produced it instead of defending the code. Tests and accessibility go in as requirements, not as a final phase. And architecture is chosen by the blast radius of a mistake, not by the elegance of the diagram.
